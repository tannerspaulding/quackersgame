Privacy in Quackers

The short version:

  • We never ask for your real name, where you live or your school.
  • The game keeps your duck progress so we can see how people play and make it better.
  • Nobody can tell who you are from it.
  • You can switch it off or delete it in Settings.

For parents

Quackers is a free game made by Mitchell, a kid, with help from his dad, Tanner. There are no ads, no accounts, no in-game purchases with real money and no third-party trackers. We keep a small amount of play data so we can see which worlds and ducks kids enjoy. Data privacy comes first, always. This page says exactly what that data is.

What we never collect

  • IP addresses. Our game server doesn't save them, and it writes no logs.
  • Real names, email addresses or phone numbers.
  • Exact location, school, contacts or photos.
  • Anything typed, except a leaderboard nickname or an idea for the Idea Box, if your child chooses to add one.
  • Advertising IDs or tracking cookies.

What the game keeps for each device

The first time the game runs, it makes a random ID for that device. It's a string of letters and numbers that isn't linked to a name, account or email. Under that ID we keep:

  • When the game was first and last played, and how many days it was played.
  • Country and state.
  • Whether it's a phone, tablet or computer, and whether it's the home-screen app or a browser. The browser's details stay on the device; only that one word is sent.
  • Game progress: which ducks were found, worlds reached and finished, Pond Points, best level in each mini game, and the last world and screen.
  • Which days the device played, kept for 90 days for a players-per-day chart.
  • A leaderboard nickname, if your child adds one. It goes through a name filter, and Tanner can hide any name.
  • Idea Box ideas and votes (see below).
  • A random share code for the friend link, and how many friends joined with it.

Totals that aren't linked to anyone

Some numbers are only counted as totals, so they can't be traced back to a player:

  • How many players were in each city each day. The city is worked out roughly by Cloudflare from the visit and is never saved with a player's data.
  • How long each play session lasted, and the hour and weekday it started, under a separate random ID that isn't linked to the player.
  • How often each duck is found, mini game tries and wins, shop items bought and Pond Points earned.

The Idea Box

Kids can suggest new ducks and worlds and vote on other players' ideas. A filter blocks bad words, links, numbers and personal details. Then a grown-up reads every idea before anyone else can see or vote on it. Ideas show without a name, and the link between an idea and its device is removed when the month ends. Ideas that aren't approved have their text erased right away.

The friend link

A player can share a link to the game with a friend. The link has a random code in it, which isn't the device ID and says nothing about the player. When a new player opens the link and finds their first duck, the player who shared it gets a bonus duck. Once that happens, we keep only that the new device was counted, not who invited it.

Turning it off

  • In the game, open Settings (the gear) and switch Share game stats off. The game then sends nothing. The leaderboard, Idea Box and friend link need stats sharing, so they turn off too.
  • If the browser has Do Not Track turned on, the game sends nothing.
  • Your child can still play every world and mini game with sharing off. Progress is saved on the device itself.

Deleting your child's data

  • In Settings, tap Delete my data twice. That deletes everything kept for that device on our server, and clears the game's progress on the device.
  • Data for a device that hasn't played for 12 months is deleted automatically.
  • To ask us to delete data another way, email tannerspaulding81@gmail.com.

Where it's kept and who sees it

The data is stored with Cloudflare, on Tanner's own account. Only Tanner can see the stats page, and it's password protected. We never sell or share the data with anyone. Cloudflare runs the server, so it may keep its own short-lived records of requests to run its service.
